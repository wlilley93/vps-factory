# Product Lead, Legal Engine — in-scope sections (The role · How we work · Who we're looking for · What success looks like)

## The role

You'd be our first hire beyond the four of us. That means direct exposure to some genuinely novel product work, and a real chance to test yourself on problems that don't have obvious answers.

One thing defines this role: you turn a customer's real problem into a specification a machine can build from. Ambiguity gets resolved once, by you, not constantly re-surfaced over time.

AI agents have collapsed the cost of writing software to almost nothing. They haven't touched the cost of deciding what to build, proving it's safe, or convincing a law firm to trust it. The binding constraint has moved from generating code to understanding, verifying and judging — and that's still human work, and what we want from this role.

## How we work

We don't do sprints. A formally-verified specification does the alignment work standing meetings would — decisions get written down once — and single artefacts provide the transparency.

You run the discovery conversation and come out with these structured, machine-readable briefs, precise enough that an agent-assisted engineer, or an agent, can build against it without going back to the customer.

That means:

Sitting with our customers and getting to the bottom of what they actually need. Not what they say they need.

Turning those conversations into structured specifications our engineering process can build from directly. If you've used Claude Code and have opinions on how to write specs for it, that's the kind of thing we're looking for.

Looking across our customers and spotting where three different requirements are secretly the same product, or where they're not. Helping to make the call on which we invest in.

Building the business case for what we build and what we don't; NPV, second-order effects etc. You should enjoy this kind of thinking.

Helping decide what gets built next. You'll sit between what Sam and Lee hear from clients and what Edu is building — turning conversations into the specs, demos and prototypes that set his day-to-day priorities.

Supporting with our compliance and procurement paperwork. DPIAs, RFP responses, InfoSec questionnaires. Not glamorous, but this is where deals get won or lost, and it needs someone who can hold their own with a General Counsel or a Head of IT.

Preparing the client-facing materials that go alongside a build. Training, onboarding, first-90-days documentation.

## What success looks like

Six months in, we'd expect you to have:

Built out standardised product specs across everything we offer.

Cut Edu's client meetings by more than half.

Taken over DPIAs, RFP responses, and the rest of the compliance workload.

Taken the lead on how we prioritise what to build.

Earned the trust of a real chunk of our client base — they should be bringing you their ideas, not the other way round.

## Who we're looking for

You'll be great in this role if:

You've built products before and have views on what makes them good. You think in responsibilities, not features.

You're technical enough to write your own specs and iterate them until they work. Not so technical that you'd rather be building the thing yourself.

You've held your own in senior stakeholder rooms.

You care about clarity and usability at a level that most people find slightly tiresome.

You'd rather have five things on your plate and figure out which two matter than have one narrow remit.

You're keen to experience what it's like inside a small AI company that's early, moving fast, and already selling to real clients.

We're hiring for attitude and potential more than exact track record. If the job interests you but you don't tick every box, we still want to hear from you.

You probably won't enjoy this role if you want a backlog handed to you and a definition of done written by someone else; if you'd rather write a feature list than resolve the ambiguity underneath it; or if you treat a compliance questionnaire as paperwork to clear rather than a promise to a client.
