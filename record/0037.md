# Record 0037

This repository ceases to be a jurisdiction.

It held a genesis, a statute book of 30 instruments, a citation series and a constitutional
gate. All five of its statutes governed its own files and none governed work product — not
through carelessness, but because the enforceable rule language is `pathForbidden(scope)` and
`recordRequired(scope)` over a git diff, so every statute it was capable of enacting was
necessarily about its own directories. Two of the five were already vacuous, entrenched, and
therefore unrepairable.

A factory that produces verdicts about other people's requirements does not need custody of
its own source tree by constitutional means. Article 9 already holds that the record is not
the product; this applies the same cut one layer down.

What moves: adjudication. When S2 needs a question decided it now asks the Vibe Justice
System, and the answer lands in the court's docket with the court's citation. Verified
end to end — a live run filed [2026] VJS 7 through 10 into the court, and a second run
applied all four in 1.5 seconds without a bench sitting, against 84 seconds for the first.
Res judicata holds across the process boundary.

What stays: `src/precedent/questions.ts`. Extracting questions from an IR is knowledge about
the IR, which is this factory's domain. Everything after extraction is the court's.

What is deleted: 731 lines of court and kernel machinery that duplicated the court's own, and
had already diverged from it in eight of twelve shared modules — including, for several hours,
a safety fix that stopped one copy reporting a Lean compile failure as a constitutional
denial while the other went on doing it.

Sign-off no longer files a ruling. A `signoff:` entry was never a decision on a question; it
was an event, already recorded with the signer, the time and the exact IR hash it binds. It
existed only because filing a ruling used to be the only way to obtain a citation. Nothing is
weakened: `requireSignoffCurrent` still gates S5–S7 on that file and that hash.

**What is genuinely lost, stated plainly:** this repository can no longer say "a change here
was refused by an instrument." That is a real reduction in enforcement and a deliberate one.
Its self-protection is now tests, which is where it effectively was — the citator corruption
of 2026-08-23 was caught by a test, and the statute enacted in response to it could not have
fired on it.

The old jurisdiction is archived unaltered under `archive/vps-jurisdiction/`, citable as
history, exactly as v1 and v2 are preserved in the court's own repository.

Retiring it also dissolves a knot nothing else could: [2026] VPS 30 was entrenched over
`lean/Vjs/`, so that directory could never be renamed — `entrenched_immune` is a theorem.
A jurisdiction cannot repoint an entrenched statute, but a sovereign can retire the whole
jurisdiction, and that is the only lawful exit from the trap.

## Dissolution

The pre-commit hook is uninstalled, and that act stands outside the jurisdiction it ends.

The gate ran `vps gate --staged`; retiring the jurisdiction removed the `gate` command, so
the hook failed and refused the very commit that retires it. There is no lawful sequence out
of this from inside: the enforcement cannot authorise its own removal, and [2026] VPS 3
forbade touching `gate/` outright, so no record could have cured it either.

This is the exact mirror of the court's commencement (its record/0001), where the founding
commit had to be made with `--no-verify` because [2026] VJS 5 denied it and the denial was
unsatisfiable at genesis. A constitution's beginning and its end are both acts of the
sovereign, and neither is governed by the constitution itself. Both are named rather than
quietly done.
