# Record 0038

The `context` noun role is removed from the IR.

`Noun.role` offered three values — `subject`, `requirementItem`, `context` — and the third
was never implemented anywhere. It appears in the zod enum and in the schema text handed to
the drafter, and nowhere else: not in codegen, not in the checks, not in the TS mirror, and
not in any of the four cases this factory has run.

It could not have worked if a model had used it. `Predicate.params` is `.length(2)`, so a
predicate takes exactly a subject and an item; there is no third parameter for a context
noun to bind to, and `Expr.field` paths are `param.field` with a single dot, so nothing in
the expression language could have referred to one. A drafted context noun would validate,
survive every check, generate no Lean, and mean nothing.

That is worse than dead code, because the drafter is told about it. `IR_SCHEMA_TEXT` in
stages.ts listed the role to the model, so a live draft could legitimately produce a noun
that silently carries no force — and the ensemble would not flag it, because all three
drafts could agree on emitting it.

This is a removal, not a change to the rule language. Nothing that could be expressed
yesterday cannot be expressed today; a capability that was announced but absent is now
neither. So it needs no plan revision and no ruling, which wiring a third predicate
parameter would have needed, and rightly: that would be an extension of the IR under
CLAUDE.md 5, with a decidability argument and both example vectors to carry.

The case for a context parameter is real and is not being refused here — `allOf` over a
finite item list with one flat subject cannot express a requirement that depends on
anything else. It is deferred to the trigger the kernel's own plan names: grow the language
when a real case hits the limit, and let the shape of that failure choose the constructor.
Four cases have not hit it.
