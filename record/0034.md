# Record 0034

The Book Protection Act lost its subject within the hour, and the lesson from record/0033
had to be learned twice.

record/0033 established that an entrenched statute cannot follow its subject: [2026] VPS 2
was left guarding `lean/Vps/` after the kernel became a dependency, and because it is
entrenched it could not be repointed, only supplemented by [2026] VPS 30 over `lean/Vjs/`.

Minutes later, tidying a naming confusion, `lean/Vjs/` was renamed to `lean/Book/`. The
build stayed green. Most tests stayed green. The only symptom was the gate quietly ALLOWING
a hand edit to the generated statute book that it had DENIED moments before — because VPS 30
still pointed at the old path and VPS 30, being entrenched, could not be moved either.

The rename is reverted. `lean/Vjs/` keeps its historically confusing name, and
`lean/Vjs/README.md` now says at the point of temptation why it cannot be changed. A
misleading directory name is cosmetic; law guarding a path that no longer exists is
structural, and the failure is silent in the permissive direction.

**Entrenchment makes directory names constitutional.** That is the whole finding. The
protection an entrenched statute provides is welded to the literal string in its scope, so
moving the thing protected does not move the protection — it removes it, with no error, no
failing build, and a gate that simply starts saying yes.

If such a path must ever move, the order is: enact the new statute first, verify it denies,
then move, and accept a permanently vacuous instrument on the book. Never move first.
