# Record 0030

`book_lawful`'s growth: measured, not operated on.

`book_lawful` is a nested `Lawful.enact` term — one layer per instrument, two `by decide`
calls per layer, each traversing the whole book — so its cost grows roughly quadratically in
rulings filed, and every case Foundry runs files about four. That was raised as work to do
before the general-theorem tier, on the reasoning that both phases increase ruling volume.

Measured before acting. A cold `lake build Vps` at 29 instruments, 56 `by decide` calls:
**1.7 seconds**, against the 120-second timeout `enact()` imposes. The margin is roughly
seventy-fold.

Decision: do not operate. The fix — a decidable `lawfulB` checker plus a one-time soundness
theorem, collapsing `book_lawful` to a single application — is constitutional surgery on a
kernel that is currently verified, and would have to be enacted upstream in VPS, re-proved,
and re-vendored. Performing that on a 1.7-second build is precisely the machinery-about-
machinery that ASSESSMENT.md identifies as what killed VJS v2: work whose only subject is
the system's own plumbing, undertaken because it was on a list rather than because anything
was failing.

The trigger is automated rather than remembered: `test/book-build-budget.test.ts` fails when
the rebuild passes 30 seconds — a quarter of enact()'s timeout, so there is room to do the
work deliberately rather than under pressure. Its failure message names the fix and where it
must be enacted.

Worth stating plainly, because it is the whole point: when the budget does blow, the symptom
will not look like slowness. `enact()` treats a timeout as "the enactment never happened",
so a book that has outgrown its shape presents as rulings mysteriously failing to file.
